SECTION 1: WORKING HOURS AND ATTENDANCE

Official working hours are 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM, Monday to Friday, with a one-hour lunch break between 1:00 PM and 2:00 PM.

Employees are expected to be at their desks or workstations by 8:00 AM. Arriving after 8:30 AM counts as late arrival.

An employee who is late more than three times in one month will receive a written warning from the line manager.

Employees who will be absent or late must inform their line manager by phone call or WhatsApp message before 8:30 AM on the same day.

Overtime must be approved in writing by the line manager before it is worked. Approved overtime is paid at 1.5 times the normal hourly rate on weekdays.

Work done on a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday at the request of management is paid at twice the normal hourly rate or compensated with a day off in lieu.

Employees working in shifts, such as security, customer support and operations staff, follow the shift roster published every Friday for the following week.

Shift swaps between employees must be approved by the supervisor at least 24 hours before the shift.

Employees must clock in and out using the biometric attendance system or the attendance app. Clocking in for another employee is gross misconduct.

Employees who forget to clock in must report to their line manager on the same day so the record can be corrected.

SECTION 2: ANNUAL LEAVE

Every confirmed employee is entitled to 20 working days of paid annual leave per year.

Employees on probation may take leave only after confirmation, except in emergencies approved by the Human Resources Manager.

Leave requests must be submitted at least 7 days in advance through the HR portal or the leave request form.

Leave approval is at the discretion of the line manager, based on workload and the number of colleagues already on leave in the department.

Employees may carry over a maximum of 5 unused leave days to the next year. Any extra days not used by 31 March are lost.

Employees who are not able to take leave because of business needs may be paid for the unused days, with approval from the Managing Director.

Public holidays that fall within an approved leave period are not counted as leave days.

An employee must not travel or commit to travel bookings before the leave request is approved in writing.

An employee who does not return on the approved resumption date without informing the company will be treated as absent without leave.

Employees who resign or are terminated are paid for accrued unused leave days, calculated up to the last working day.

SECTION 3: PUBLIC HOLIDAYS

The company observes all public holidays declared by the Federal Government of Nigeria.

These include New Year's Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Workers' Day on 1 May, Democracy Day on 12 June, Independence Day on 1 October, Christmas Day and Boxing Day.

Islamic holidays such as Eid-el-Fitr, Eid-el-Kabir and Maulud are observed on the days declared by the Federal Government.

When the Federal Government declares an extra public holiday, the company will notify employees by email within 24 hours.

When a public holiday falls on a Saturday or Sunday, the next working day is observed only if the Federal Government declares it.

The company is normally closed from 25 December to 1 January. Staff in essential services will work on a roster and receive time off in lieu.

SECTION 4: SICK LEAVE

Every employee is entitled to 12 days of paid sick leave per year.

A medical certificate is required for absences longer than 2 days.

The medical certificate must be issued by a registered hospital or clinic and must state the dates of rest recommended by the doctor.

An employee who is sick must inform the line manager before 8:30 AM on the first day of absence.

Sick leave is not carried over from one year to the next.

Abuse of sick leave, including false medical certificates, will lead to disciplinary action and may lead to dismissal.

An employee who is sick for more than 30 consecutive days may be asked to attend a medical review by the company doctor or the Health Maintenance Organisation (HMO) provider.

Employees injured at work must report to their supervisor immediately and to the HR department within 24 hours.

SECTION 5: MATERNITY, PATERNITY AND FAMILY LEAVE

Female employees are entitled to 16 weeks of maternity leave on full pay, which can start up to 4 weeks before the expected delivery date.

Maternity leave applies to each birth, including multiple births, and does not reduce annual leave.

A pregnant employee must notify HR in writing at least 8 weeks before the expected delivery date and attach a medical certificate.

After maternity leave, nursing mothers are allowed one hour per day for breastfeeding for six months after resumption.

Male employees are entitled to 10 working days of paid paternity leave, to be taken within one month of the birth of the child.

Employees who adopt a child are entitled to 8 weeks of paid adoption leave.

Employees are entitled to 5 days of paid compassionate leave on the death of a spouse, child, parent, sibling, or parent-in-law.

Employees are entitled to 3 days of paid leave for their own wedding and 1 day for the wedding of a child or sibling.

An employee who needs to attend a family emergency may apply for up to 3 days of emergency leave, to be approved by the line manager.

Employees who are Muslims or Christians may apply for leave to perform Hajj or the Holy Land pilgrimage. This leave is unpaid, except that annual leave may be used.

SECTION 6: STUDY AND EXAMINATION LEAVE

Employees who study part-time may apply for up to 10 days of paid examination leave per year, supported by the examination timetable from the school.

Employees must apply for examination leave at least 14 days before the first paper.

Study leave of more than 30 days is unpaid and must be approved by the Managing Director.

The company may support professional certifications such as ICAN, ACCA, CIPM, PMP and CFA, subject to approval by the department head and the HR Manager.

Employees who receive company-sponsored training must stay for at least 12 months after completion, or pay back part of the cost.

SECTION 7: DRESS CODE

Formal business attire is required from Monday to Thursday. Male employees wear shirts, trousers and a tie or a suit, and female employees wear corporate dresses, skirts or trousers.

Casual clothes are allowed on Fridays.

Traditional Nigerian attire such as native wear, Ankara, kaftan and agbada is welcome on any day, as long as it is neat and professional.

Employees must not wear ripped jeans, flip-flops, singlets, crop tops or clothes with offensive writing to the office.

Employees who meet clients must wear formal attire even on Fridays.

Staff who work in warehouses, plants and sites must wear the protective clothing and safety boots supplied by the company.

Employees may wear religious dress, including hijab and turban, as long as it does not create a safety risk.

SECTION 8: REMOTE WORK AND HYBRID WORK

Employees may work from home on Fridays with manager approval.

An employee who wants to work from home must send a request to the line manager by 5:00 PM on the day before.

Remote employees must be reachable on phone, email and the company chat between 8:00 AM and 5:00 PM.

Remote employees must join all scheduled video meetings with their camera on, unless the data connection is poor.

The company may provide a monthly data allowance of N15,000 to approved remote workers.

Where there is a serious flooding, protest, fuel scarcity or traffic situation that makes travel unsafe, management may declare a remote working day by email.

Employees must not work from public places with open Wi-Fi when handling confidential company data.

Remote work is a privilege and may be withdrawn if performance or availability drops.

SECTION 9: EXPENSE CLAIMS AND REIMBURSEMENT

Expense claims must be submitted within 14 days with receipts attached.

Claims are paid at the end of the month.

Claims without receipts will be rejected, except for small transport expenses of less than N5,000, which can be supported by a signed note.

All claims must be approved by the line manager before being sent to the Finance department.

Employees on official trips are paid a daily allowance, called per diem, of N25,000 within Nigeria for staff and N50,000 for managers.

Per diem for international trips is paid in US dollars at the rate set by the Finance department, depending on the destination.

Employees who travel by air must use economy class, except for Directors who may use business class on flights longer than 4 hours.

Hotel accommodation must be booked through the company travel desk or from the approved hotel list. The maximum rate is N80,000 per night in Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt, and N60,000 in other cities.

Employees must not claim personal expenses, entertainment of family members or fines for traffic offences.

Fuel and generator expenses for official purposes must be recorded in the fuel log and approved by the line manager.

Employees who give false claims will face disciplinary action and must refund the amount.

SECTION 10: SALARY AND PAYMENT

Salaries are paid by bank transfer on the 25th day of every month. If the 25th falls on a weekend or public holiday, payment is made on the previous working day.

Every employee must have a valid bank account in their own name and must give the details to HR on resumption.

A payslip is sent to the employee's work email every month showing basic salary, allowances, tax and pension deductions.

Employee salary is made up of basic salary, housing allowance, transport allowance and other allowances, as stated in the employment letter.

Salary information is confidential. Employees must not discuss their salaries with other colleagues.

Any error in a payslip must be reported to the Payroll Officer within 5 working days.

Salary overpayments will be recovered from the next salary, and the employee will be informed in writing before deduction.

The company pays a 13th month salary in December to confirmed employees. Employees who joined during the year receive it in proportion to months worked.

Annual salary reviews are done in January based on the performance appraisal and the financial position of the company.

SECTION 11: TAX AND STATUTORY DEDUCTIONS

The company deducts Pay As You Earn (PAYE) income tax from the salary of every employee and remits it to the relevant State Internal Revenue Service every month.

Employees must provide their Tax Identification Number (TIN) to HR within 30 days of joining.

The company contributes to the Pension Reform Act scheme. The employer pays 10% and the employee pays 8% of monthly basic salary, housing and transport allowance.

Every employee must choose a Pension Fund Administrator (PFA) and open a Retirement Savings Account (RSA). Employees must give their RSA PIN to HR.

The company pays the Industrial Training Fund (ITF) contribution of 1% of annual payroll and the Nigeria Social Insurance Trust Fund (NSITF) contribution of 1% of monthly payroll.

Employees who earn more than the threshold set by law contribute to the National Housing Fund (NHF) at 2.5% of basic salary, where applicable.

The company provides a tax deduction certificate to employees at the end of the year.

SECTION 12: EMPLOYEE BENEFITS AND HEALTH INSURANCE

All confirmed employees are enrolled in a Health Maintenance Organisation (HMO) plan. The plan covers the employee, a spouse and up to four children below 18 years.

Employees on probation are covered by the HMO from the first day of work.

Employees must use the hospitals listed under their HMO plan. Treatment outside the HMO list will not be paid for, except in emergencies.

Emergency treatment at any hospital must be reported to the HMO within 48 hours.

The company provides group life insurance equal to three times the annual basic salary of the employee.

The company provides group personal accident insurance for all staff.

Employees who work for more than 3 years receive a long service award on every 5-year anniversary.

The company provides a monthly transport allowance as part of the salary. Staff in some locations receive a shuttle bus service.

Employees may apply for a staff loan after 12 months of service. The loan must not be more than three months of net salary and is repaid in up to 12 monthly instalments.

The company may give a cooperative society loan or salary advance in emergencies, at the discretion of the Finance Manager.

The company provides lunch or a meal allowance to staff on duty after 7:00 PM.

SECTION 13: RECRUITMENT AND PROBATION

All vacancies are advertised internally for 5 working days before being opened to external candidates.

Recruitment is based on merit, and the company does not discriminate on grounds of tribe, religion, gender, state of origin, disability or marital status.

New employees must provide credentials, two guarantors, a recent passport photograph, a valid ID, and a police character certificate if requested.

The company verifies the certificates, employment history and addresses of candidates before the employment is confirmed.

Guarantors must be employed persons of good standing, such as civil servants of grade level 10 and above, or senior professionals, and must not be relatives.

New employees serve a probation period of 6 months. The company can extend it by up to 3 months.

A performance review is held at the end of probation. The employee will receive a confirmation letter if the review is satisfactory.

During probation, either party may end the employment with one week's written notice.

Graduates who come for the National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) are accepted as corps members and are paid a monthly allowance in addition to the federal allowance.

Interns and trainees are engaged for a fixed period and receive a monthly stipend. An internship is not a guarantee of employment.

SECTION 14: PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT

Performance is assessed twice a year, in June and December, using the agreed key performance indicators (KPIs).

Every employee will agree objectives with the line manager within the first month of the year.

Ratings are on a scale of 1 to 5. A rating of 1 or 2 means the employee will be placed on a performance improvement plan of 90 days.

An employee who does not improve during the performance improvement plan may be moved to another role or dismissed.

Employees with a rating of 4 or 5 are eligible for a bonus and consideration for promotion.

Promotion depends on performance, availability of a position, years of experience and approval by the management committee.

Employees may appeal their performance rating to the HR Manager within 7 days of receiving it.

SECTION 15: CODE OF CONDUCT AND DISCIPLINE

All employees must behave with honesty, integrity and respect to colleagues, customers and the public.

Gross misconduct includes theft, fraud, bribery, violence, sexual harassment, drug abuse at work, falsifying documents and leaking confidential information.

An employee found guilty of gross misconduct may be summarily dismissed without notice or payment in lieu of notice.

For minor offences, the disciplinary steps are a verbal warning, a first written warning, a final written warning and then dismissal.

The employee will be given a chance to respond to any allegation in writing within 48 hours before a decision is made.

An employee may be suspended with or without pay for up to 14 days while an investigation is going on.

Employees may bring a colleague or a union representative to a disciplinary hearing.

Employees must not come to work under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs, and must not smoke inside company premises.

Employees must not use their position to ask for gifts, favours or payment from customers, vendors or colleagues.

Employees must not run a business that competes with the company or that takes up their working time without written approval.

SECTION 16: ANTI-BRIBERY, GIFTS AND CONFLICT OF INTEREST

The company has zero tolerance for bribery and corruption, in line with the Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Act.

Employees must not offer, give, ask for or accept bribes in any form, including cash, gifts, favours or "something for the boys".

Gifts and hospitality with a value of more than N20,000 must be declared to the Compliance Officer and may be refused or returned.

Cash gifts must never be accepted from a vendor, customer or contractor.

Employees must declare any personal or family interest in a vendor, customer or competitor to their manager and to the Compliance Officer.

A staff member must not take part in any decision to employ, contract or promote a family member.

Employees are not allowed to make political donations in the name of the company.

SECTION 17: SEXUAL HARASSMENT AND WORKPLACE RESPECT

The company has zero tolerance for sexual harassment, bullying, discrimination and any abuse of power.

Sexual harassment includes unwanted touching, comments, jokes, messages, requests for sexual favours or any behaviour that makes someone feel unsafe.

Complaints can be sent to the HR Manager or the Ethics Hotline, and employees may report anonymously.

All complaints will be investigated within 14 days, and the complainant will be protected from retaliation.

Employees found guilty of harassment will face disciplinary action up to dismissal, and where the law requires, the matter will be reported to the police.

Managers must report any harassment they see or hear about, even if the victim has not made a formal complaint.

SECTION 18: WHISTLEBLOWING

Employees are encouraged to report fraud, corruption, safety risks and breaches of policy using the whistleblowing hotline, email or by contacting the Head of Compliance.

The identity of a whistleblower will be kept confidential as far as the law allows.

No employee will be punished, demoted or dismissed for reporting a concern in good faith.

A report made with false intent to harm a colleague is a disciplinary offence.

SECTION 19: GRIEVANCE PROCEDURE

An employee with a complaint should first discuss it with the line manager within 5 working days of the issue.

If the matter is not solved, the employee may submit a written grievance to the HR Manager, who will respond within 10 working days.

If the employee is not satisfied with the HR decision, they may appeal to the Managing Director, whose decision is final in the company.

An employee has the right to take a matter to the Ministry of Labour or the National Industrial Court if it cannot be solved internally.

SECTION 20: RESIGNATION, NOTICE AND TERMINATION

A confirmed employee who wants to resign must give one month of written notice, or one month's salary in lieu of notice.

Senior managers and directors must give three months of written notice.

The company must give the same notice period when it ends the employment of an employee, except in cases of gross misconduct.

During the notice period, the employee must continue to work and hand over all duties, files and company property.

Employees who are made redundant will receive a severance package of two weeks' pay for each year of service, in line with the Labour Act and company practice.

Before an employee leaves, they must return the ID card, laptop, phone, keys, uniform, books and other company property to the HR department.

The final salary and any benefits are paid within 30 days after the clearance form has been signed by all departments.

An employee who leaves after 5 years of service may qualify for a gratuity under the company scheme, if they were not dismissed for misconduct.

The company will give a certificate of service or a reference letter on request. The reference will only confirm the position held and the period of service.

SECTION 21: INFORMATION SECURITY

Employees must keep passwords private and must change them every 90 days.

Passwords must have at least 10 characters and include upper case letters, lower case letters, numbers and symbols.

Multi-factor authentication is required for email, the HR portal and the finance system.

Employees must lock their computer screen when leaving their desk, even for a short time.

Employees must not install unauthorised software or connect personal USB drives to company computers.

Company data must be stored only on approved company systems and not on personal email or personal cloud storage.

Employees must report a lost or stolen laptop or phone to the IT Helpdesk and their line manager within 2 hours.

Phishing emails should be reported to the IT Security team and not forwarded to colleagues.

The company may monitor the use of its computers, email, internet and telephone systems for security and compliance reasons.

Employees must not share confidential company information on social media, WhatsApp groups or with journalists.

SECTION 22: DATA PROTECTION AND PRIVACY

The company complies with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and the regulations of the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.

Personal data of customers and employees must be collected only for lawful and specific purposes, and kept only as long as needed.

Employees must not access, copy or share personal data unless it is necessary for their job.

A data breach must be reported to the Data Protection Officer within 24 hours of discovery. The company will inform the Nigeria Data Protection Commission within 72 hours where required.

Customers have the right to ask what personal data the company has about them, and to ask for it to be corrected or deleted.

Employee records are kept confidential by HR and shared only with persons who need them or when required by law.

SECTION 23: USE OF COMPANY ASSETS

Company laptops, phones, vehicles and equipment are for official use. Limited personal use is allowed if it does not affect work.

Employees are responsible for company equipment given to them and may be asked to pay for loss or damage caused by negligence.

Company vehicles must be used only for official duties, and drivers must keep a logbook of trips and fuel.

Employees must not drive company vehicles under the influence of alcohol, and must follow the Federal Road Safety Corps (FRSC) rules, including the use of seat belts.

Employees must switch off lights, air conditioners and equipment at the end of the day to save power and diesel.

Company stationery, internet and printers must not be used for personal business.

SECTION 24: HEALTH, SAFETY AND ENVIRONMENT

The company provides a safe working environment in line with the Factories Act and the Employees' Compensation Act.

Employees must follow safety instructions, wear protective equipment and report any hazard to their supervisor.

Fire drills are held twice a year. All employees must take part and know the assembly point and exit route.

Each floor has a first aid box and trained first aiders. Incidents and near misses must be recorded in the incident register.

Smoking is not allowed inside any company building or vehicle.

During power outages, the company runs generators for essential services. Generators must be operated and refuelled only by trained persons.

Employees must not bring flammable items, weapons or illegal items to the company premises.

The company supports waste reduction, recycling and the careful use of water and electricity.

Employees must follow any public health instruction from the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) and the Ministry of Health.

SECTION 25: SECURITY AND ACCESS TO PREMISES

Every employee must wear the company ID card visibly at all times inside the premises.

Visitors must sign in at the reception, show a valid ID and wear a visitor badge, and must be accompanied by the host employee.

Employees must not allow anyone to enter restricted areas, such as the server room, without authorisation.

Employees who work late must inform security and sign the late register.

Lost ID cards must be reported to HR immediately, and a replacement fee of N5,000 will apply.

Employees travelling to high-risk areas must follow the travel safety advice given by the Security Manager.

SECTION 26: TRAINING AND DEVELOPMENT

Every employee has an annual training budget agreed with the line manager as part of the development plan.

New employees attend a 2-week induction programme covering company policies, systems, safety and anti-corruption.

Compliance training on anti-bribery, data protection and information security is compulsory every year.

Employees who do not complete compulsory training on time may not receive their bonus.

The company runs mentoring and coaching programmes for high-performing staff.

Employees may use the company library and online learning platform during working hours, with the approval of their manager.

SECTION 27: PROCUREMENT AND VENDORS

Purchases above N500,000 require at least three quotations from registered vendors.

Purchases above N5,000,000 must be approved by the Procurement Committee and signed by two Directors.

Vendors must be registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC), hold a valid tax clearance certificate and pass the company's due diligence checks.

Payments to vendors are made by bank transfer to the account name that matches the registered business name. Cash payments are not allowed, except small petty cash.

Employees must not split a purchase into smaller parts to avoid approval limits.

SECTION 28: PETTY CASH AND FINANCIAL CONTROLS

Petty cash is kept by the Finance department and is used only for small official expenses of up to N20,000 per item.

All petty cash payments must have a voucher approved by the line manager.

Cash handling employees must count and reconcile the cash daily and report any difference immediately.

Cash payments of more than N500,000 are not allowed. Larger payments must be made by bank transfer.

Employees must follow the company's anti-money laundering rules and report any suspicious transaction to the Compliance Officer.

Two signatories are required on all bank transfers above N1,000,000.

SECTION 29: CUSTOMER SERVICE AND COMMUNICATION

Customer calls must be answered within three rings, and emails must be answered within 24 hours.

Customer complaints must be logged in the complaints system and solved within 5 working days. Serious complaints are escalated to the department head.

Only authorised spokespersons may speak to the media for the company. Other employees must refer media enquiries to the Corporate Communications department.

Employees must be polite and professional in all emails, messages and calls, whether with customers or colleagues.

Employees should not post anything on social media that harms the name of the company or that shares confidential information.

SECTION 30: STAFF MEETINGS AND COMMUNICATION

A weekly team meeting is held every Monday at 9:00 AM, and employees must attend unless they have approved leave or a client appointment.

A monthly town hall meeting is held on the last Friday of the month, where management shares company updates.

Official communication is by company email and the staff portal. WhatsApp groups can be used for quick updates but not for formal approvals.

Meetings must start and end on time, and have an agenda and written action points.

SECTION 31: STAFF WELFARE AND WELLNESS

The company gives an employee assistance programme with free confidential counselling by phone.

Employees may apply for a short break of up to 3 days after very difficult events, subject to approval from HR.

The company holds an annual end-of-year party and family day for employees.

Birthdays and work anniversaries are celebrated, and each employee receives a gift card of N10,000 on their birthday.

The company supports a staff cooperative society, and membership is voluntary.

SECTION 32: UNION AND LABOUR RELATIONS

Employees have the right to join a trade union of their choice, according to the Trade Unions Act.

The company will consult recognised unions on important changes to working conditions.

Strikes and industrial action must follow the procedures set by the law, and the company will try to settle any dispute through dialogue first.

SECTION 33: EQUAL OPPORTUNITY AND INCLUSION

The company gives equal opportunity to all employees and candidates, regardless of gender, tribe, religion, age, disability or state of origin.

The company will make reasonable changes to the workplace and role for employees with disabilities.

Employees are expected to respect the different cultures and religions of their colleagues.

Prayer breaks of up to 15 minutes are allowed for Muslim employees during working hours, and the line manager will agree the arrangement with the employee.

SECTION 34: POLICY REVIEW

These policies apply to all employees, contractors and interns of the company.

The policies are reviewed every year by the HR department and approved by the Managing Director.

The company may change any policy at any time to follow new laws or business needs, and will inform employees by email.

Where a policy conflicts with Nigerian law, the law will apply.

Employees who are not sure about any policy should ask the HR department for clarification.
